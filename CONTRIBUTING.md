# Contributing to Teen Service Project

TSP welcomes corrections, research sources, data quality concerns, program feedback, and practical improvements. Prioritize youth leadership, community strengths, equitable access, transparent methods, and accurate language about development status.

## Choose a useful route

- **Research suggestions:** open an issue using the Research suggestion template. Provide a verifiable source URL, publisher, date if known, relevance, and methodological limitations. An issue is a suggestion, not verification of the evidence.
- **Resource feedback:** use the Resource feedback template for clarity, access barriers, proposed adaptations, or critiques of program assumptions. State the local context without identifying individuals. Do not present an untested idea as established practice.
- **Technical or accessibility problems:** use the Technical problem template. Describe the page, what happened, what you expected, and relevant device/browser information. Do not include private screenshots or credentials.
- **Direct edits:** use a branch and pull request, describe the reason and sources, and wait for checks. Routine browser editing is covered in EDITING-GUIDE.md.

GitHub is a public collaboration workspace. **Never post youth participant names, photographs, contact details, sensitive accounts, private records, tokens, or confidential datasets.** A confidential feedback process must be established outside public issues before sensitive information is collected. Avoid collecting information that is not needed.

## Research standards

Record provenance and verification status in the relevant project inventory. Explain definitions, dates, coverage, missingness, uncertainty, selection, comparison groups, permissions, and limits. Distinguish causal claims from descriptive findings. Flag contradictory evidence and data errors. Do not fabricate sources or copy a statistic without checking its basis.

Do not commit raw/processed research data by default. Public data sharing requires review of rights, confidentiality, documentation, and disclosure risk. Analytical outputs must be reviewed before moving them into public website assets. Keep analysis out of routine website rendering.

## Program resources and community adaptations

Explain the audience, context, proposed or tested status, what changed, and what you learned. Include criticism and unsuccessful approaches where useful. Original TSP materials should be reviewed before being republished. Document contributions and revisions without claiming organizational endorsement or universal effectiveness.

## Corrections and review

Identify the specific page or data field, the concern, proposed correction, and supporting evidence. Material corrections should include a dated public note. A maintainer checks accuracy, access, permissions, privacy, and consistency before publication. Do not promise acceptance or event participation through an issue.

## Licensing is undecided

No blanket open-source or Creative Commons license has been applied. Do not contribute third-party materials without appropriate rights or add a license on the initiative’s behalf. Research code, publications, and program materials may receive different licenses later; see docs/LICENSING.md.
